# Neutral handoff prompt for Pro

I am giving you a package of historical XI artifacts from different stages of the design. Some may be current, superseded, partial implementations, design notes, duplicates, or contradictory.

Do not assume every artifact is authoritative. Do not operationalize every named role merely because it exists. Do not begin by inventing a grand architecture.

Start with archaeology:

1. Recover what the supplied artifacts directly establish: invariants, roles, interfaces, implementations, runtime laws, provenance behavior, validation behavior, scoring/refraction concepts, orchestration concepts, state transitions, and failure behavior.
2. Mark important conclusions as **EVIDENCED**, **INFERRED**, or **OPEN**.
3. Identify exact duplicates, version evolution, contradictions, dead code, malformed files, overlapping responsibilities, and places where later material appears to narrow or supersede earlier material.
4. Propagate into the unknown where the evidence strongly suggests a useful missing structure, but label new synthesis as new synthesis rather than recovered fact.
5. Only after presenting the recovered system and unresolved questions, propose the smallest coherent operationalization. Preserve mechanisms that materially improve containment, observability, reproducibility, evidence integrity, bounded recovery, or useful orchestration. Leave decorative/redundant machinery out.
6. For every major architectural decision, say whether it was recovered, inferred, or newly chosen, and cite the artifact(s) that support it.

The goal is to discover the strongest system latent in the supplied material and make it real without laundering design choices into historical facts.

Important provenance note: `00_raw/original_user_bundle.zip` is the untouched source bundle. `01_materials/` and `02_prism_expanded/` are convenience views. If they disagree, inspect the raw ZIP.

Do not write implementation code until you have shown me the recovered architecture, contradictions, open questions, and your proposed resolution strategy.
